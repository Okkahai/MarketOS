# MarketOS correctness and research-architecture audit

Date: 2026-11-05. Commit audited: `fe7bb60` (main, after PRs #1 to #12).
Scope: whole repository. No code was changed. Line numbers refer to that commit.

How the findings were checked: I read every module on the decision path, ran the existing suite
against a local PostgreSQL 16 and Redis (310 passed), and wrote seven throwaway reproduction tests
outside the repo (they reuse the repo's own fixtures from `tests/integration/test_trading.py`).
Each reproduction asserts today's behaviour, so "passes" means the defect is real. They are listed
under each finding and become the regression tests of the fix, with the assertion inverted.

Severity: **P0** silently invalidates research conclusions or P&L in normal operation.
**P1** materially biases results under common conditions. **P2** edge case, parity gap or
latent risk. Each finding is also tagged **[software]** (the code does not do what it claims) or
**[financial]** (the code does what it claims, but the assumption is wrong for research).

### Lifecycle (what actually happens)

1. `ingest_stock_daily` (hourly) stores Tiingo **raw** OHLC per trading date, `ts` = date 00:00 UTC,
   `available_at` = 20:00 New York that date (`market/bars.py:192-193`). Crypto: Coinbase 1d and 1m,
   `available_at` = close + 5 s.
2. News jobs (15 min) store articles with `available_at = max(published_at, collected_at)`
   (`news/normalize.py:106`).
3. `cluster_events` (10 min) attaches unclustered articles to open events; the `events` row is the
   latest state (a cache); `state_as_of` rebuilds it from articles available at a time.
4. `ai_analysis` (15 min) picks open events by cached importance, builds a context `as_of = now`,
   runs triage and analysis (rules by default), journals signals with
   `reference_price` = last daily close available at `now` (`ai/analyze.py:304-305`).
5. `paper_cycle` (5 min): stops/targets on daily bars, a risk verdict per new signal, pending orders
   filled at the open of the first bar that opens after the decision and is already available,
   hourly snapshot.
6. `evaluate_signals` (hourly) writes one immutable row per signal and horizon once the calendar
   horizon has passed.
7. Backtests replay steps 4 to 5 over stored data with a fixed clock.

## B. Verified findings

### B.1 Confirmed defects

#### F1 (hypothesis E). Forecasts are credited with price moves that finished before the decision. **P0 [software + financial]**

- **Where:** `backend/app/analytics/evaluate.py:70-73` (start = `sig.reference_price`, window
  starts after `reference_price_ts`); the start price comes from `ai/analyze.py:304-305` and
  `ai/context.py:75-76` (last close available at decision time).
- **Root cause:** the evaluation starts from the last close the analyst saw, not from the first
  price at which the decision could have been acted on.
- **Reproduction:** the repo's own test asserts it. `tests/integration/test_analytics.py:45-58`
  makes a BUY at Monday 21:00 UTC (the US market closed at 20:00 UTC) and expects the "1d" result to
  be Friday close 100 to Monday close 102, +2%, "correct". That whole move happened before the
  signal. My probe `test_E` adds a Tuesday bar (open 102, close 97): the forecast is still scored
  correct (+2%) while a trade at the first tradable price lost 4.9%.
- **Expected:** forecast return measured from the first executable price after the decision
  (next session open) to the horizon end. The pre-decision move can be stored separately as a
  diagnostic ("drift before entry").
- **Consequence:** a news-following strategy, which is exactly what the rules analyst is, buys
  after good news that has usually already moved the price. Its hit rate is inflated by the very
  move that triggered it. For signals made after the close or over a weekend, the 1d horizon is
  almost entirely pre-decision. Today's hit-rate numbers cannot answer the research question.
- **Correction:** a versioned evaluation methodology (v2) in a new append-only table; v1 rows stay
  and are labelled "measured from last close seen". See D and M2.
- **Regression test:** probe `test_E` inverted: v2 entry = Tuesday open 102, return = -4.9%,
  direction wrong; v1 row unchanged.

#### F2 (hypothesis B). The evaluation queue starves after the first 500 live signals. **P0 [software]**

- **Where:** `backend/app/analytics/evaluate.py:108-114` and `:24`.
- **Root cause:** every run selects the 500 oldest live signals (`order_by(generated_at)
  .limit(500)`) with no filter for "still needs a horizon". Completed signals are skipped only
  inside the loop (`:117`), after they have used up the 500 slots. Signals that can never be
  evaluated (F3) occupy slots forever too. The whole evaluation table is also loaded into memory on
  every run (`:103-107`).
- **Reproduction:** probe `test_B` sets `PER_RUN = 1`, makes two signals with 40 days of bars and
  runs the job five times: the first signal gets its 4 rows, the second never gets any.
- **Expected:** every signal whose horizon has elapsed is eventually evaluated, newest included.
- **Consequence:** once the journal passes 500 live signals (the rules analyst can produce up to
  15 per 15-minute run), the Analytics page freezes on the oldest signals without any error or alert.
- **Correction:** select signals missing at least one due horizon with `NOT EXISTS` against the
  evaluation table, keyset-paginated by `(generated_at, id)`; give permanently unevaluable cases a
  terminal status (F3) so they leave the queue. Index `performance_evaluations(signal_id)` exists
  via the unique key.
- **Regression test:** probe `test_B` inverted, plus a test with 3 pages of signals where the
  newest page is evaluated in the first run after the older ones are complete.

#### F3. Weekend and holiday signals never get short-horizon evaluations, and nothing records why. **P1 [software]**

- **Where:** `backend/app/analytics/evaluate.py:67-72`; model `models/evaluation.py:22-50` has no
  `status` column, although `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §4 and §10 say it does (`insufficient_data`).
- **Root cause:** horizons are calendar days. A Saturday or Sunday signal's 1d window contains no
  trading day, so `bars` is empty and the function returns `None` forever.
- **Reproduction:** probe `test_weekend` (Saturday signal, 60 days later): `1d` is missing,
  `3d` and `7d` exist. A Friday-evening signal gets a 1d result of Thursday close to Friday close,
  which is again entirely pre-decision (F1).
- **Consequence:** hit rates are computed on a silently selected subset, and these signals feed F2.
- **Correction:** horizons in trading sessions from a session calendar (M2); explicit terminal
  statuses `insufficient_data` and `not_applicable` written as rows.
- **Regression test:** weekend, holiday (US July 3 2026 observed holiday), and "data gap longer
  than tolerance" cases each produce a row with the right status.

#### F4 (hypothesis A). An expired order can still fill. **P1 [software]**

- **Where:** `backend/app/trading/engine.py:159-173`, `bars_after` at `:59-77`.
- **Root cause:** `fill_pending` looks for a bar first and checks `expires_at` only when there is
  none. `bars_after` has no upper bound at the expiry time.
- **Reproduction:** probe `test_A`: order at Monday 21:00 UTC, expiry Thursday 21:00 UTC (72 h);
  the paper cycle does not run until Saturday; it fills at Friday's open, after expiry. In live use
  this needs the worker or beat to be down across the expiry (or a cycle that skipped); in a
  backtest it happens whenever `--step-hours` is longer than the expiry or a data gap spans it.
- **Expected:** a bar is eligible only if its open time is before `expires_at`; otherwise the
  order is expired with an effective time of `expires_at`, not of whenever the engine noticed.
- **Consequence:** fills at prices outside the validity window, usually after worker outages.
- **Correction:** filter eligible bars by `session_open(bar) < expires_at` before choosing; expire
  when the first candidate is at or past expiry.
- **Regression test:** probe `test_A` inverted; boundary bar opening exactly at `expires_at`;
  backtest with `step_hours=96`.

#### F5 (hypothesis C). Fills are back-dated relative to when they became knowable, and the risk counters read them that way. **P1 [software]**

- **Where:** `backend/app/trading/engine.py:164-167` (`executed_at` = 09:30 New York of the fill
  bar), while the bar becomes usable at 20:00 New York (`market/bars.py:192-193`) and the fill is
  written by a later cycle. Exits use another convention, `executed_at = bar.available_at`
  (`engine.py:196-201`). Risk counters filter on `executed_at` and ignore pending orders
  (`trading/state.py:118-136`).
- **Reproduction:** probe `test_C`: daily trade cap 1, three BUY signals in one cycle: three
  approvals. After the fills arrive (stamped Tuesday 13:30 UTC, written Wednesday 01:00 UTC) a new
  signal still sees "0 of 1 today", because the fills belong to the previous UTC day and pending
  orders were never counted.
- **Answer to the hypothesis:** the price itself is not lookahead (it is the open of a bar that
  started after the decision), but the record claims the portfolio held the position from 09:30,
  hours before the system could know the fill price. Any question of the form "what did the
  portfolio look like at time T" answered from `trades.executed_at` disagrees with what the engine
  actually knew at T, and with the immutable snapshot taken at T.
- **Consequence:** the daily trade cap (risk rule 11) is ineffective for stocks; the cooldown
  (rule 10) does not see a pending order in the same asset, so a second buy within 24 h is allowed
  (still bounded by the position cap); point-in-time reconstruction of the book is not possible.
- **Correction:** an explicit execution-time model (D.3): keep `executed_at` as market time of the
  price, add `price_available_at` and `recorded_at` (simulated clock at write), count risk limits
  by decision time including pending orders, and answer "as of T" questions with `recorded_at <= T`.
- **Regression test:** probe `test_C` inverted (second and third signals rejected by the cap);
  cooldown with a pending order; "book as of T" equals the snapshot at T.

#### F6 (hypothesis G). The entry bar is never checked for stop-loss or take-profit. **P1 [software]**

- **Where:** `backend/app/trading/engine.py:184` calls `bars_after(..., pos.last_entry_at, ...)`,
  which keeps bars whose open is strictly after `last_entry_at` (`:77`); `last_entry_at` is the
  fill bar's own open (`trading/execute.py:265-273`), so the fill bar is always excluded. The
  existing test `tests/integration/test_trading.py:218-219` codifies this with the comment "low is
  below the stop but before/at the entry".
- **Root cause:** a wrong reading of daily OHLC. For a fill at the open, the open is the first
  print of the session, so the bar's low and high happened at or after the entry. The bar can be
  checked. What daily data cannot say is the order of the low and the high, which matters only when
  both levels are inside the bar (already resolved as "stop wins").
- **Reproduction:** probe `test_G`: Tuesday open 100 (entry), low 90, stop 92.05: position stays open.
- **Consequence:** optimistic bias. Entry-day stop-outs never happen, so losses are understated.
- **Correction:** include the fill bar for open-filled positions; stop fills at the stop (the open
  is above it by construction); same-bar stop and target stays pessimistic; record the assumption
  on the trade. Crypto positions fill on a 1m bar but exits are checked on daily bars
  (`engine.py:184` uses `INTERVAL = "1d"` for all classes), so the rest of the crypto entry day is
  skipped and the daily bar cannot be used because it contains pre-entry prices. That part is a
  limitation (L7), fixed later by checking 1m bars or accepted and labelled.
- **Historical records:** existing open positions should not be stopped out retroactively by a
  new exit dated in the past. Apply the rule only to positions whose opening decision has the new
  `engine_version` (already stored on `risk_decisions`), so no migration is needed.
- **Regression test:** probe `test_G` inverted; double-touch entry bar; open == low edge case;
  a position opened under `risk-1` keeps the old rule.

#### F7 (hypothesis F). Raw, unadjusted prices drive evaluation, stops, valuation and indicators. **P1, P0 on any split [financial]**

- **Where:** `backend/app/providers/tiingo.py:43-51` stores raw `open/high/low/close`; `adj_close`
  is stored but read only by the market API (`api/routes/market.py:173`). Tiingo's `divCash` and
  `splitFactor` fields are discarded. Indicators for decisions use raw closes
  (`ai/context.py:62-69`).
- **Reproduction:** probe `test_F`: a 4:1 split (open 25 after 100) fires the stop and books a
  realised loss of about 300 on a 400 position.
- **Consequence:** the universe is full of past splitters (AAPL 2020, TSLA 2020 and 2022, AMZN and
  GOOGL 2022, NVDA 2021 and 2024). A split during an open position creates a fake loss, a fake
  "correct" SELL or AVOID evaluation, and spikes RSI, 5-day return and the SMA gap, which change the
  analyst's decisions. Dividends are ignored, which slightly understates stock returns. `adj_close`
  is only refreshed inside the 5-day overlap, and every dividend creates new revision rows there
  because `_same` compares `adj_close` (`market/repository.py:131-133`).
- **Correction:** store corporate actions point-in-time (M4), apply splits to open positions as
  explicit ledger events (quantity, average cost, stop and target all scaled), credit dividends as
  `cash_ledger` rows of a new kind, and compute indicators and evaluation returns on a series
  adjusted only with actions known at `as_of`.
- **Regression test:** split mid-position (no stop, quantity x4, value unchanged), dividend
  credited, evaluation across a split, indicator continuity across a split.

#### F8 (hypothesis H, part). The analytics averages are not direction-aware. **P1 [software]**

- **Where:** `backend/app/api/routes/analytics.py:92-103`.
- **Root cause:** `avg_return_pct` and `avg_excess_pct` average the asset's raw return over BUY,
  SELL, REDUCE, AVOID and HOLD together; a correct AVOID (price fell) lowers the average.
  Benchmark assets are evaluated against themselves: a SPY signal always has excess 0, and BTC
  signals likewise (`analytics/evaluate.py:21,77-85`). All live signals are pooled; there is no
  split by strategy, model or action.
- **Correction:** signed return (direction x return), per action and per strategy; exclude
  self-benchmarked rows from excess statistics.
- **Regression test:** a BUY +2% and a correct AVOID -2% give signed average +2%, not 0.

#### F9. Calendar-unaware freshness rejects every stock entry the day after a holiday. **P2 [software]**

- **Where:** `backend/app/trading/risk.py:287-289` measures age from `reference_price_ts`, the bar
  date at 00:00 UTC. After a Monday holiday, at Tuesday 21:00 UTC the newest usable close is
  Friday's: 117 h > `PAPER_MAX_PRICE_AGE_HOURS_STOCK` 100 h. Every stock BUY that evening is
  rejected as stale. Related: `trading/state.py:53-58` prefers a 1m crypto bar up to 54 h old over
  a fresher daily bar when valuing.
- **Correction:** measure staleness in missed sessions (M2 calendar); pick the most recent mark
  across intervals.

#### F10 (hypothesis I, part). Context snapshots include non-point-in-time fields of past events. **P2 [software]**

- **Where:** `backend/app/ai/context.py:117-158` reads `Event.title`, `category`, `first_seen_at`
  and `available_at` of past events from the cached latest state, which `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §4
  itself says is not a point-in-time record.
- **Consequence:** in a backtest, a past event's title can come from an article collected after
  `as_of`. The rules analyst does not read `related_past_events` (no reference in
  `ai/rules_llm.py`), so today's decisions are unaffected, but the snapshot claim "nothing from the
  future" is false and an LLM or ML strategy would read it.
- **Correction:** build past events with `state_as_of`; add a test that injects a late article
  into a past event.

#### F11. Backtest and live select events differently. **P2 [software]**

- **Where:** live: open events by cached importance and `last_updated_at`
  (`ai/analyze.py:127-136`); backtest: events whose *first* availability is inside the window,
  importance rebuilt as of `t` (`backtest/runner.py:66-84`). An event that keeps receiving articles
  after its window is analysed live but skipped in a replay.
- **Consequence:** a backtest is not the same policy as live paper trading, so they cannot be
  compared directly.
- **Correction:** one shared, point-in-time selection function used by both.

### B.2 Architectural limitations (by design today, block the research goal)

- **L1 No strategy identity.** Signals carry no strategy id, version or configuration hash; the
  analyser's model string (`rules-analysis-1`, `core/config.py:17-18`) does not change when the word
  lists change. Every portfolio of a mode consumes every signal of that mode
  (`trading/engine.py:86-96`), so two strategies cannot run side by side.
- **L2 Horizon is a label (hypothesis D).** The rules analyst always says `7d`
  (`ai/rules_llm.py:182`); trading never reads `time_horizon`; evaluation scores all four horizons
  for every signal (`analytics/evaluate.py:116`). "Hit rate at 1d" is not the hit rate of 1-day
  forecasts. Position lifetime is decided only by stop, target or a later SELL.
- **L3 Confidence is a formula, not a probability.** `0.30 + 0.25|tone| + 0.25 importance + 0.10
  event confidence (+0.05)`, capped at 0.85 (`ai/rules_llm.py:116-121`). The UI shows it as
  "confidence 0.70". It is uncalibrated.
- **L4 No baselines.** Only total return of SPY, BTC and cash over the snapshot window
  (`analytics/portfolio.py:259-266`). No buy-and-hold, random-entry, always-buy or momentum
  reference, no beta or exposure adjustment, no confidence intervals.
- **L5 Pseudo-replication.** One event produces up to 3 signals with identical tone
  (`ai/rules_llm.py:130-132`); a market-wide event produces SPY, QQQ and BTC signals at once
  (`ai/context.py:194-195`); the same event can be re-analysed every 6 hours. Signals are counted
  as independent samples.
- **L6 Backfilled bars use theoretical availability.** A bar first fetched weeks later is stamped
  with its nominal 20:00 availability (`market/repository.py:102-103`), and its values are those of
  the fetch date. Fine for raw EOD prices, but backtests over backfilled history are "theoretically
  available", not "as collected". This should be labelled, not hidden.
- **L7 Daily bars cannot order intraday events.** Stops versus targets in one bar, intraday stop
  breaches that recover, crypto exits after 1m entries. Any rule here is a convention and must be
  recorded as such on the trade.
- **L8 No session calendar.** Weekends, holidays and early closes are handled by tolerances
  (`GAP_TOLERANCE` 4 days, freshness 100 h), not by knowing the sessions.
- **L9 Events are built once, live.** Backtests replay whatever the live clustering decided;
  changing the clustering rules does not change replays unless events are rebuilt, and
  `event_assets` rows are deleted and rewritten (`events/build.py:211`).
- **L10 Documentation drift.** `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` describes a `status` column on evaluations,
  `insufficient_data` rows and "every signal older than its horizon has evaluations" (§4, §10);
  none exist. Entry-bar exits are described as "after the last entry" without the rationale being
  correct.

### B.3 Unverified risks (plausible from the code, not reproduced)

- **U1** Tiingo's `splitFactor` and `divCash` fields on the free plan, and the behaviour of
  `adjClose` on ex-dates. Must be checked with Gün's live key before M4.
- **U2** Re-analysis on portfolio-only changes: the request hash includes the portfolio
  (`ai/context.py:51-54`, `ai/analyze.py:198`), so an event with no new articles may produce a new
  analysis and new signals every 6 hours when cash or positions change.
- **U3** Event membership with hindsight: within one clustering batch, articles are ordered by
  `published_at` (`events/build.py:96-104`), so a late-collected article can bridge two articles
  into one event; `state_as_of` hides the late article but the grouping it caused stays.
- **U4** Overlapping paper cycles under a slow run: the unique verdict key prevents double
  decisions, but concurrent `fill_pending` on the same order is not guarded by a row lock.
- **U5** Crypto 1m history retention and gaps; Coinbase reachability on Gün's network.

## C. Research methodology review

The question "does strategy S add value beyond baselines" needs three separate measurements. Today
they are mixed together.

| Measurement | Question | Unit | Definition proposed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Forecast evaluation | Does the signal predict the direction or excess return? | one (event, asset, strategy) forecast | entry = first executable price after decision (next session open), exit = close of the Nth session after entry, return and benchmark-relative return, signed by the forecast direction |
| Executable signal performance | Would trading every signal in a standard way have made money after costs? | one standard trade per signal | same entry/exit, fixed unit size, the configured slippage and fees, no portfolio constraints |
| Portfolio P&L | What does the full system (risk rules, cash, caps) earn? | portfolio | as today, but one portfolio per strategy under identical rules |

What must change:

1. **Point of entry.** Measure from the first tradable price (F1). Keep the pre-decision drift as
   its own number; it tells whether the strategy is late.
2. **Horizon in sessions**, and evaluate the horizon each forecast declared as its primary
   result; other horizons are secondary diagnostics (L2).
3. **Benchmark-relative targets.** Directional accuracy is dominated by market drift (stocks rise
   more often than not). The primary target should be excess return over SPY (stocks) or BTC
   (crypto), and hit rate should be reported against the base rate (share of up moves over the same
   windows), not against 50%.
4. **Independence.** Count one forecast per (event, asset, strategy), the first one; cluster
   standard errors by trading day, because news days are correlated (L5).
5. **Uncertainty.** Report bootstrap confidence intervals. For scale: telling a 55% hit rate from
   50% at 95% confidence needs about 385 independent forecasts (1.96 x 0.5 / sqrt(n) = 0.05). With a
   few signals a day this is months of data, and the UI should say so.
6. **Calibration.** Do not call the score a probability. Once enough data exists, show a
   reliability table (score bins against observed frequency) and the Brier score; until then label
   it "score (uncalibrated)".
7. **Baselines under identical conditions.** Cash; buy-and-hold SPY; equal-weight universe;
   random entry matched to the strategy's trade frequency, holding period and asset mix (seeded, so
   reproducible); always-buy-on-news; a simple technical rule. Same data gate, same execution model,
   same costs, same risk rules.
8. **Exposure versus alpha.** Report beta to the benchmark and the return not explained by it,
   and the average exposure (share invested), so a strategy that is simply long in a rising market
   is not mistaken for skill.
9. **Out of sample.** Fix thresholds and word lists, record their hash, and evaluate only data
   after that point. Any tuning restarts the out-of-sample clock (walk-forward). Report every
   variant tried, not only the best.
10. **Corrections without rewriting.** Methodology changes create new evaluation rows with a
    `methodology_version`; v1 rows stay, labelled.

## D. Target architecture (smallest evolution)

Same stack, same packages, no new services. Additions only.

### D.1 Strategy layer

```
Strategy (protocol)
  id, name, version, config (JSON), config_hash, code_version
  decide(context: PointInTimeContext) -> list[Decision]   # HOLD and "no decision" included
```

- `RulesBaseline`: today's analyst, unchanged in behaviour, wrapped (it already implements
  `LlmClient`; the wrapper adds identity).
- `TechnicalBaseline`, `RandomBaseline` (seeded by strategy config and date), `BuyAndHold`,
  later `MachineLearningStrategy` and `LLMResearchStrategy`.
- New table `strategies` and a nullable `strategy_id` on new signals. Old signals cannot be updated
  (trigger), so legacy attribution is a view: `ai_analyses.model = 'rules-analysis-1'` maps to
  `RulesBaseline v1`.
- New table `strategy_runs`: one row per strategy per as-of, input context hash, counts, including
  runs that produced nothing. This is where "rejected" and "no decision" live.
- Strategies never touch portfolios; they produce signals. Each strategy gets its own portfolio
  (`portfolios.strategy_id`), and `decide()` in the engine filters signals by it.

Alternative considered: one shared portfolio with signals tagged by strategy. Rejected because
strategies would compete for cash and slots, so their P&L would depend on each other.

### D.2 Evaluation v2

New append-only table `forecast_evaluations`: `signal_id, horizon_sessions, methodology_version,
status (complete | insufficient_data | not_applicable), entry_price, entry_price_ts,
entry_available_at, exit_price, exit_price_ts, asset_return, benchmark_symbol, benchmark_return,
excess_return, signed_return, signed_excess, pre_entry_drift, mfe, mae, evaluated_at`. v1 stays.

Session calendar: derive past US sessions from stored SPY daily bars (no new dependency; enough
for evaluation, which only needs past sessions). If future sessions are ever needed (order expiry
in sessions), add `exchange_calendars` (Apache-2.0, free).

### D.3 Execution-time model

| Time | Meaning | Stored as |
|---|---|---|
| decision time | when the risk verdict was made | `risk_decisions.decided_at`, `orders.signal_time` |
| eligible after | the order may use bars that open after this | = decision time |
| price time | when the fill price printed (session open; for stops, an unknown time inside the bar) | `trades.executed_at` (kept) |
| price available | when the system could know that price | new `trades.price_available_at` = bar `available_at` |
| recorded | simulated clock when the fill was written | new `trades.recorded_at` |
| expiry | last moment a bar may open and still be used | `orders.expires_at` |

Rules: a bar is eligible if `eligible_after < open < expires_at` and `available_at <= now`.
Everything that asks "what did the system know at T" filters on `recorded_at <= T`. Risk counters
(daily cap, cooldown) count orders by decision time, pending ones included. Stops and targets
record `price_time` as the bar's session and `price_assumption` (`gap_open`, `stop_level`,
`target_level`, `double_touch_stop`).

Alternative considered: set `executed_at` to the availability time. Simpler, but it puts the
open's price at 20:00 and makes holding periods wrong. Recording both times is cheap and honest.

### D.4 Corporate actions

Table `corporate_actions (asset_id, ex_date, split_factor, cash_dividend, source, collected_at,
available_at, raw)`. Applied to open positions as explicit events (new ledger kind `dividend`, a
split adjustment record), never by editing trades. Adjusted series for indicators and evaluation
are computed at read time from actions with `available_at <= as_of`.

### D.5 Daily research workflow

One Celery chain at a fixed time after the 20:00 New York availability (for example 21:30 New
York): ingest, data-quality gate (fresh bars for each asset, news sources ran, no stuck jobs),
cluster, run enabled strategies, risk, fill, snapshot, evaluate, report. Intraday jobs stay as they
are for news. Table `daily_reports` (append-only JSON plus rendered text), sections: new hypotheses;
executed and rejected decisions with reasons; active positions; forecasts maturing today with
results; strategy and baseline comparison with intervals; exposure; missing or late data. Scores are
labelled uncalibrated until calibration exists.

### D.6 Machine learning (later, gated)

Dataset builder from stored context snapshots and bars as of each decision (no recomputation from
today's data); targets from evaluation v2 (excess return sign and size over N sessions); dataset
version = hash of rows, feature code version and target definition; time-based splits and
walk-forward; leakage tests (no feature with `available_at` after decision; no overlap of target
windows across the split); logistic regression first, gradient-boosted trees second; calibration
(Platt or isotonic) fitted on the validation fold only; model registry rows with metrics per fold.
Gate before training: an amount of independent forecasts agreed in advance (I suggest at least
1,000 event-asset forecasts over at least 6 months, both classes well represented). Below that, the
honest output is "not enough data".

### D.7 Event intelligence (measure before building)

Label about 100 real event clusters by hand once live news runs (split stories, wrong merges, wrong
tickers). Then, in order of cost: topic tags so Fed items and Tiingo articles about the Fed can join;
merge of events that share a ticker and are later found similar; novelty (first report versus
repetition); source reliability learned from evaluation v2 outcomes; filing interpretation by form
item (8-K items 2.02, 5.02, 1.01) rather than titles. Embeddings with a small local model (for
example a MiniLM sentence model on CPU, free) only if the labelled error rate shows rules are not
enough.

## F. First implementation milestone (M1), proposed for approval

Smallest set that removes confirmed defects from the engine and the evaluation queue without new
tables, new dependencies or behaviour changes to historical rows.

1. **Order expiry (F4).** `trading/engine.py` `bars_after`/`fill_pending`: only bars that open
   before `expires_at`; expire otherwise. Tests: probe A inverted, boundary bar, backtest with
   `step_hours=96`.
2. **Evaluation queue (F2).** `analytics/evaluate.py`: select signals missing a due horizon with
   `NOT EXISTS`, keyset pages, no full-table load. Tests: probe B inverted, three pages.
   Terminal statuses wait for M2 because they need a new table. Until then, signals that can never
   be evaluated stay pending, but because each run walks all pending signals in pages instead of
   stopping at the oldest 500, they can no longer block newer ones (documented as a shortcut).
3. **Entry-bar exits (F6).** `trading/engine.py` `check_exits`: include the fill bar for positions
   opened under `risk-2`; existing positions keep `risk-1` behaviour. The existing test
   `test_stop_loss_gap_fills_at_the_open_and_ignores_the_entry_bar` asserts the defect; it would be
   kept for `risk-1` positions and a new test added for `risk-2`, not deleted.
4. **Risk counters (F5, partial).** `trading/state.py` `load_state`: daily cap and cooldown count
   orders (pending and filled) by decision time instead of trades by `executed_at`. Tests: probe C
   inverted, cooldown with pending order.
5. **Analytics averages (F8).** `api/routes/analytics.py`: signed returns per action, exclude
   self-benchmarked rows, rename "confidence" to "score (uncalibrated)" in the UI. Tests: signed
   average fixture; frontend label test.
6. Docs: correct `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` drift (L10) and add an "Audit 2026-11-05" section listing
   what M1 changed and what is still open (F1 in particular, so nobody reads v1 hit rates as
   meaningful in the meantime).

Not in M1, on purpose: evaluation v2 (F1), corporate actions (F7), new timestamps (F5 full),

Risk of M1: future paper P&L becomes slightly worse (entry-day stops, fewer trades per day). That is
